## 1. The week-1 question — the script

**Deliverable: `scripts/analytics/funnel-week1.cjs`. It is UNRUN. Karam runs it.**

I cannot query production and did not try. The script is read-only and built to

### What it answers

Exactly the lines the plan asks for, plus the decomposition its decision table
needs:

| Section | Answers |
|---|---|
| q1 | auctions ended in window · met reserve · reserve not met · zero bids · still running (excluded) · never-started lots |
| q2 | bids per auction — median, mean, p25/p75/p90, max; unique bidders; 1-bid vs 2+-bid lots |

### How it is safe

- **No writes.** No `set`/`update`/`delete`/`add`/`create`/`batch` call exists in
  the file, and there is no `--apply`. This is checkable, not promised:
  `grep -nE "\.(set|update|delete|add|create)\(|\.batch\(|bulkWriter" scripts/analytics/funnel-week1.cjs`
  returns only the header comment describing the audit. Uniqueness is tracked
  with plain objects rather than `Set` specifically to keep that grep clean.
- **Refuses ambient credentials.** Exits unless `GOOGLE_APPLICATION_CREDENTIALS`
  is set; never falls back to a shell's default login.
- **Aggregates only.** Counts, sums, percentiles. No ids, names, phones,
  national ids, payment references or CliQ aliases, and deliberately **no
  `--ids` flag** (the sibling `phase1-census.cjs` has one; this one must not,
  because its output is meant for `ops/data/`). `subscriptionRequests` carries
  `userName`, `userEmail`, `transferFullName`, `transferPhone` — none is read.
- **Models no costs and states no targets.** Delivery cost, sourcing and ad
  spend are not in Firestore; putting them in the script would print an
  assumption next to measured facts. The model lives in §3 of this document,
  where its assumptions are labelled.

### Validation I could do without production

`node --check` passes, and I unit-tested the pure logic (32 assertions, all
green) against the cases most likely to corrupt the headline number — in
particular the `endTime: null` guard. `src/types.ts:139-143` warns that a
`first_bid` lot that never received a bid carries `endTime: null`, and
`null < Date.now()` is `true`; an unguarded comparison would file every

script exists to measure. Also covered: `paid` without `paymentVerified` must
not count as awaiting-shipment, the 24h SLA boundary, and the payment-deadline
`>=` boundary that mirrors the server's `<=`.

**What validation does NOT cover:** the script has never touched a real
database. Field-presence assumptions on live documents, query cost, and the
`listUsers` permission are unproven until Karam runs it. It degrades rather
than crashes if the key lacks the Auth role — registrations then report
`unavailable`, explicitly "missing, not zero", and every other section still
prints.

### Two things the script will surface that the plan's decision table does not anticipate

1. **`reserve_not_met` is not one bucket, it is two, and they have opposite
   fixes.** `functions/settlement.js:126-141` returns `reserve_not_met` with
   `reserveUnverifiable` when a reserve was *intended* but its amount is missing
   from `auctionSecrets` — because `createListing` wrote it from the browser and
   the admin-only rule denied it, the error was swallowed to a `console.warn`,
   and the lot went live with no retrievable reserve. The plan's table says
   "auctions are not meeting reserve → lower reserve prices — easy". For these
   lots that is the **wrong fix**: nothing was too expensive, a write was
   denied. The script splits them.
2. **A sold auction with no order document** is a winner who was never invoiced
   — the anomaly `repairEndedAuctionOrder` exists for. That is a bug, not a
   refusal to pay, and counting it as non-payment would manufacture evidence for
   the plan's "existential" hypothesis. Reported separately.

### The failed-handover caveat — read this before quoting q5

**Firestore records no seller-default state at all.** There is no seller-side
sweep, no 48-hour seller-confirmation timer, and the server never advances
`shippingStatus` past `"not_started"` — an absent seller simply leaves the order
untouched forever. The plan's "1 in 3 transactions failed because a seller
became unreachable" is **not stored anywhere**.

So q5 is derived, using the product's own rule (`bucketOrder` + `isOverdue`,
`src/utils/fulfillmentQueues.ts`, surfaced as `seller_hasnt_prepped` in
`src/utils/actionQueue.ts`): a **paid** order still in `awaiting_shipment` past
the 24h SLA. It **undercounts twice**: it is point-in-time, so a lot that
stalled and was rescued by hand no longer shows; and its clock runs on
`updatedAt`, which unrelated writes bump, silently resetting it (the caveat is
written into `fulfillmentQueues.ts:98-112` itself). **Treat q5 as a floor, and
do not compare it directly to the stated 1-in-3 without saying so.** Task 18 in
the plan — the 48-hour seller timer — is what would make this a measured fact
instead of an inference. That is the strongest argument for shipping it.

### Finding: the plan's "registration to bid 40%" is not a conversion rate

The plan's target table lists *"Registration to bid — Today 40% — hold at 40%+"*.
40% of 313 is 125, which is 126 bids. So the figure is **bids ÷ registrations**
— bid *events* over *people*. It is not a conversion rate and cannot be held as
one.

The true registration→bidder rate is **capped at 7.7%**, because bidding is
gated on a paid membership and only 24 people bought one. At most 24 people
placed those 126 bids — roughly 5.25 bids per bidder, which is a very different
business from "40% of registrants bid". This matters: a target to "hold at
40%+" is a target to hold a number that does not mean what it says, and it makes
the funnel look five times healthier at the bid stage than it is. Script q2
(`distinctBiddersAllTime`) and q6 settle it.

### Finding: visits are not measurable today, by anyone

Not a missing export — a missing instrument.
- `analytics_events` requires `isSignedIn()` (`firestore.rules:476-480`), so an
  anonymous visitor can never write one. The signed-in-only subset cannot
  produce a visit count.
- Landing-page events go to `window.dataLayer` **and nowhere else**. The code
  says so itself (`src/landing/landingAnalytics.ts:1-6`): "intentionally NOT
  wired to analyticsService".
- There is no GA4 tag — no `gtag` in `index.html` or `src/main.tsx`.

So the 21,000 figure cannot be reproduced or refuted from this repo, and
**visit → registration cannot be computed at all** until GA4 or a Meta export
exists (`ops/DATA_REQUIREMENTS.md` #4 and #6). Any "registration rate improved"
claim at the end of week 2 — which is what gates plan task 24, the influencer
spend decision — currently has **no denominator**. That is a gating problem for
a spending decision, and it is fixable in an afternoon by installing GA4.

### Template

**Cost inputs are ASSUMPTIONS, and one is a floor.** The plan says the 10%
commission earns "about 0.6 JOD per transaction — against 6 JOD of delivery
logistics **and the staff time to coordinate it**". Staff time is *on top* of
the 6 JOD, so every contribution figure below is optimistic.

**Membership attribution is undecided.** `ops/KPI_DASHBOARD_SPEC.md:30` still
says `[FILL]`. At 30 JOD/month over ~16 completed sales/month
`[plan 2026-09-27]` it is ≈1.88 JOD per sale. I have **excluded it from the
table below** and show it separately, because attributing a membership pool to
individual auctions flatters each lot with revenue it did not generate. Decision
needed (see §6).

### The distribution problem — the whole argument in one table

The plan's core economic claim is that "a 1 JOD auction consumes the same
operational effort as a 200 JOD one". Costs are flat; revenue is proportional.
Delivery/handling held at 6 JOD; membership excluded.

| Hammer (JOD) | Take @10% | **(a)** no delivery fee | **(b)** 6 JOD fee charged |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.10 | **−5.90** | +0.10 |
| 6 *(stated ASV)* | 0.60 | **−5.40** | +0.60 |
| 20 *(proposed floor)* | 2.00 | **−4.00** | +2.00 |
| 50 *(plan target)* | 5.00 | **−1.00** | +5.00 |
| **60** | 6.00 | **0.00** ← break-even | +6.00 |
| 80 *(plan target)* | 8.00 | +2.00 | +8.00 |
| 200 | 20.00 | +14.00 | +20.00 |

**At the stated ASV of ~6 JOD, every completed sale destroys about 5.40 JOD of
value before any ad spend, sourcing cost or staff time.** Volume makes it worse,
not better. The plan is right that this is the economics problem nobody named.

**Three consequences the averages hide:**

1. **Under model (a) the break-even hammer is 60 JOD.** That is *above* the
   plan's 50 JOD ASV target and **triple the proposed 20 JOD minimum**. The 20
   JOD floor does not reach profitability — it reduces the loss from 5.40 to
   4.00 JOD per lot. Presenting the 20 JOD minimum as the fix to unit economics
   would be wrong under the model CLAUDE.md describes.
2. **Under model (b) every lot is positive**, because the fee makes delivery
   cost-neutral and contribution reverts to the bare commission. The 20 JOD
   floor then earns +2.00.
3. **Mean ASV is the wrong statistic here.** With ~4 sales, one 200 JOD lot
   moves the mean by 50 JOD and the median not at all. The script reports both
   deliberately. A rising mean with a flat median means one lucky lot, not a
   fixed business — and that is exactly the misreading that would justify
   resuming spend too early.

**Membership, for completeness:** adding 1.88 JOD/sale moves the 6 JOD case from
−5.40 to −3.52. Still deeply negative. **The membership cannot rescue the unit
economics at the current ASV** — worth stating plainly, because it is sometimes
argued as if it could.

### The delivery fee — the repo contradicts itself three ways

This is Karam's open decision (`D-108`) and I am not picking a side. But the
decision is messier than two options, and one fact changes it:

| Position | Says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CLAUDE.md | 5% + 5% + membership. **No delivery fee.** | `CLAUDE.md:8` |
| 60-day plan task 14 | **6 JOD** across every screen + updated legal terms | `[plan 2026-09-27]` |
| **Live product copy** | **"~2–4 JD"**, or **free pickup** | `src/components/HowItWorksView.tsx:75-76` |
| **The code** | **No delivery fee exists.** `totalDue` = hammer + 5% only | `functions/settlement.js:250-254` |

**VERIFIED: there is no delivery-fee field anywhere in the codebase.** A search
for `deliveryFee`, `shippingFee`, `deliveryCost`, `deliveryCharge` and their
Arabic equivalents across `src/`, `functions/` and `public/` returns **zero**
hits. Nothing is charged for delivery at checkout today.

Two things follow that affect the decision:

- **The plan's description of the current state does not match this repo.** The
  plan says "the information pages currently state 6 JOD". What this repo's
  shipped source states is **2–4 JD** (`HowItWorksView.tsx:75-76`). I did not
  fetch the live site, so I cannot say which is on mazzado.com right now — but
  if the plan's premise about today's copy is stale, task 14's framing as "make
  the screens consistent with the 6 JOD they already say" needs rechecking
  before a lawyer is paid to review wording. **The exposure is real either way**:
  customer-facing copy promises a delivery arrangement at a stated price, and
  the terms enumerate fees without it.
- **Scenario (b)'s revenue is not guaranteed, and nobody has measured the
  opt-out.** The same line already offers **free self-collection** ("Pick up
  from our location"). A buyer who takes it pays no delivery fee, so model (b)
  collapses toward model (a) for that share of orders. The real contribution is
  `(1 − selfCollectionRate) × 6 JOD` — and the self-collection rate is
  **unmeasured**; `order.deliveryMethod` (`'hand' | 'courier'`,
  `src/types.ts:492`) is where it would live. **Deciding the fee without that
  number is deciding blind**, and the plan's own open question "Does
  self-collection stay as a free option?" is therefore not a side issue — it
  determines whether the 6 JOD fee fixes the P&L or only part of it.
- A 6 JOD fee on a 1 JOD lot is 600% of item value. The fee fixes the arithmetic
  and creates an abandonment risk **concentrated on exactly the cheap lots the
  20 JOD minimum is meant to remove** — the two policies interact, and shipping
  the fee without the floor is the worst ordering.

## 4. The membership question (`D-106`)

CLAUDE.md says do not propose removing it without auction data. I am not
proposing either direction. Below is what the week-1 data must show, with
thresholds set in advance so the answer is arithmetic rather than argument.

**The methodological problem, stated honestly:** bidding is gated on
membership, so **every bidder is a member**. There is no control group, and
therefore no way to measure whether the membership improves winner-payment
behaviour from existing data alone. Any rule that depends on comparing members
to non-members is unanswerable by definition. The rules below use only what is
observable, plus one deliberate test.

### Inputs — all produced by `funnel-week1.cjs`

| Symbol | Script field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `M` | q6 `payingMembersAllTime` | people who paid |
| `A` | q6 `memberActivationRate` | share of payers who then placed ≥1 bid |
| `R` | q6 `membershipRevenueAllTime_JOD` | what the barrier collected |
| `C` | q4 `commissionEarned_*` (per month) | what commission earned |
| `W` | q3 `winnerPaymentRate` | share of winners who paid |
| `N` | q7 `registrationToPaidMember` | share of registrants who paid |

### Rule 1 — the filter's own terms (decisive on its own)

> **If `payingMembersWhoNeverBid / M` > 0.30 → the membership is not a
> seriousness filter and must be replaced by ID + OTP verification.**

A third of payers never bidding means the barrier is not selecting for
seriousness; it is a toll collected from people who then do nothing, while
excluding the 92.3% who refused to pay. The plan already names the stronger
replacement: national ID + OTP-verified phone, which "cost the honest user
nothing". **This rule fires regardless of the revenue arithmetic below.**

If that ratio is < 0.10, the filter is doing something real and Rule 2 decides.

### Rule 2 — the break-even bidder lift

Removing the barrier gains the commission on sales from the extra bidders and
loses `R` per month. The required lift is:

> **`k_breakeven = 1 + (monthly membership revenue ÷ monthly commission)`**

- **`k_breakeven ≤ 1.5` → REMOVE.** You need only a 50% lift in bidders, while
  the barrier currently excludes 92.3% of registrants — headroom of up to ~13×.
  A 50% lift is close to certain.
- **`k_breakeven ≥ 4.0` → KEEP FOR NOW, and fix ASV first.** At low ASV the
  membership really is a material share of revenue, and removing it while each
  sale loses money would add volume to a loss. Revisit the moment ASV clears 50
  JOD.
- **Between 1.5 and 4.0 → run the test in Rule 3.**

At the stated figures this is computable today for illustration: 30 JOD/month
against ~16 sales × 0.6 JOD ≈ 9.6 JOD/month commission gives
`k_breakeven ≈ 4.1` — **KEEP, just over the line**. But at the plan's target of 80 sales
at 50 JOD, commission is ~400 JOD/month and `k_breakeven ≈ 1.075` — a 7.5% lift.
**The membership's defensibility collapses as ASV rises.** The membership
question is therefore *not independent of* the ASV question, and resolving ASV
first resolves this one almost automatically. That is an argument for sequencing,
and it is why "held, pending data" has been the right call.

### Rule 3 — the test, if the rules are ambiguous

The plan's own middle path, run as a real experiment:
- **Hypothesis:** the paid barrier, not the catalogue, causes the 7.7%
  registration→member rate.
- **Change:** free first bid behind mandatory ID + OTP verification; membership
  applies from the second bid.
- **Primary metric:** registration → ≥1 bid. **Guardrail:** `W`, winner payment
  rate.
- **Minimum sample:** 150 new registrations per arm.
- **Stop rule:** abort if `W` falls more than 15 points below its pre-test
  baseline — that is the filter's only real job failing, and it is the one
  outcome that would be expensive to learn slowly.
- **Owner:** Maher (decision) · Karam (data).

**Do not run Rule 3 before ASV work lands.** Adding bidders to lots that lose
5.40 JOD each scales the loss.

## 5. Seller and buyer operations

Concrete, short, and sized for four sales a week.

### Seller side

**Sourcing pipeline** — Hamoudeh + Yousef, 2–3 categories only. Weekly sheet
(`ops/DATA_REQUIREMENTS.md` #9): merchant · date contacted · items offered ·
listed Y/N · outcome. Target 20+ committed sellers, 150+ active listings
`[plan 2026-09-27, TARGET]`. Measure time from contact → listing live; it is the
supply metric that predicts everything else.

**Listing-quality checklist** (blocking, before approval):
1. ≥1 real photo of the actual item — not a stock image. Video optional (task 11).
2. Title states make/model/capacity; condition `new`/`used` set
   (`AuctionItem.condition`).
3. Description names every known fault. An undisclosed fault is a future return
   claim (`not_as_described`) and a refund.
4. `marketPrice` set — it drives the "you saved X" reveal and the market-set-pricing
   message.
5. Category correct (drives the interests digest).
6. Ownership attestation checked (`ownershipAttested`).
7. Viewing location set, or deliberately left unset — unset renders nothing
   rather than implying a location (`src/utils/viewing.ts`).

**Starting price / reserve policy:**
- Minimum starting price 20 JOD (task 8) — **but see §3: this reduces the loss,
  it does not reach break-even under model (a). Do not present it as the fix.**
- Starting price at ~40–50% of market price; the gap is the drama that draws
  bids. Reserve, if any, at the true walk-away price.
- **Set the reserve through the `setAuctionReserve` callable, never the listing
  form.** `auctionSecrets` is admin-write-only, so a seller-side browser write is
  denied, swallowed to a `console.warn`, and the lot goes live with an
  unverifiable reserve that cannot be awarded
  (`functions/settlement.js:100-125`). This is an active trap, it silently costs
  sales, and it is the one item here I would fix this week.
- Prefer no reserve above a correctly-set starting price: a reserve that is not
  met produces an unsold lot plus a seller decision; a well-set starting price
  produces a sale.

**Seller SLA** (needs `D-105`): confirm within 24h of auction close; hand over or
dispatch within 24h of payment verification — matching the product's existing
`awaiting_shipment` threshold (`fulfillmentQueues.ts:27-31`) so the SLA and the
admin queue agree. Two consecutive misses → no further listings until a call.

### Buyer side

**Activation after membership** — the 1 JOD purchase is the moment of maximum
intent and the funnel currently does nothing with it. On approval: WhatsApp
confirmation naming **three live lots in the categories they selected**, with a
deep link to one. `payingMembersWhoNeverBid` (script q6) is the measure of how
much this is currently costing.

**First-bid nudges** — a member with zero bids after 48h gets one WhatsApp
message about a lot closing that evening. Once, not a sequence. The unified
Friday 8pm close (`D-004`) is the natural hook.

**Payment follow-up** — already automated: `paymentReminderSweep` runs every 30
minutes with `remind50Sent` / `remindFinalSent` flags, and
`paymentDefaultEnforcer` runs every 30 minutes against `paymentDeadlineAt`
(24h default). Ops should add nothing here; it should *read* q3 and find out
whether non-payment is even a real problem before building more.

**Non-paying-winner policy** (proposes `D-104`): 24h window (already the server
default) → automatic block via the existing enforcer → **second-chance offer to
the runner-up**, which is already built (`functions/secondChance.js`) and gets a
fresh payment window. Recommendation: **do not add a penalty beyond the existing
block until q3 shows non-payment is material.** The machinery exists; the
question is whether it is being exercised, and that is exactly what q3 answers.

**Delivery guarantee** (proposes `D-105`): the plan's manual escrow (task 15) is
the only mechanism that lets a failure be *undone* rather than merely made less
frequent. At 3–4 transactions a week it is a spreadsheet and one person.
**Carry the plan's own legal caution forward:** holding customer funds may fall
under Central Bank of Jordan payment-intermediary rules, and the distinction
between escrow and an unlicensed payment service needs advice **before** it
scales, not after.
